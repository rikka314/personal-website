One of the most useful skills I developed early was learning how to run AI tools locally instead of relying entirely on hosted interfaces.

Local workflows are not always convenient, but they teach valuable lessons quickly:

- environments break
- dependencies conflict
- hardware limits matter
- small configuration details change outcomes

That is exactly why they are worth learning.

## Why this matters before joining a lab

As a first-year student, I do not claim deep research experience yet. What I can offer is a strong willingness to learn and a practical comfort with the engineering side of technical work.

Local AI setup helped me build that comfort. I became more confident with:

- dependency management
- environment debugging
- reproducible setup habits
- using AI agents to accelerate implementation without losing control

## The skill behind the tooling

The real value is not just "I can run a model locally."

The real value is that local deployment forces a systems mindset. You stop treating tools as magic and start thinking about:

- what the system needs
- where failures happen
- how to recover when something changes

That mindset transfers well to research support, especially for tasks involving baselines, experiment environments, and practical troubleshooting.

## What I want next

I want to pair this engineering comfort with stronger formal knowledge in deep learning and visual perception. The combination is what excites me most:

- research questions with real technical depth
- implementation that is reliable enough to support those questions

That is the direction I want this site, my projects, and my next stage of learning to point toward.
